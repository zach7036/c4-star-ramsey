# The Ramsey number R(C₄, K₁,₃₉)

**Claim.** R(C₄, K₁,₃₉) = 46, and consequently R(C₄, W₄₀) = 46.

Write f(n) := R(C₄, K₁,ₙ). This was the *only* undetermined value of f(n) for n ≤ 41:

* Radziszowski, **Small Ramsey Numbers**, Electron. J. Combin. Dynamic Survey DS1,
  revision 18 (2026), **Table IVa** lists `39 | 46-47 | WuSR/DyDz2` — every other entry
  for n ≤ 41 is a single number (f(38) = 45, f(40) = 47, f(41) = 49).
* Boza, *Exact values and bounds for Ramsey numbers of C₄ versus a star graph*
  (arXiv:2409.12770v2, 12 June 2026) likewise gives f(38) = 45, f(40) = 47 and leaves
  f(39) as a range; its Remark 12 stops the chain "f(n) ≥ f(n−1)+1" exactly at n = 39,
  which is only consistent with f(39) being undetermined.

The proof is: three elementary lemmas that reduce the question to a single rigid
finite structure, followed by an exhaustive check of 144 canonical cases.

---

## 1. Reduction to a graph-existence question

A 2-colouring of K_N with no red C₄ and no blue K₁,ₙ is exactly a C₄-free graph G on
N vertices with δ(G) ≥ N − n (G = red graph). Hence

> f(n) = min{ N : no C₄-free graph on N vertices has δ ≥ N − n }.

Since f(39) ∈ {46, 47}:

> **f(39) = 47 ⟺ there exists a C₄-free graph on 46 vertices with δ ≥ 7.**

**Lower bound (independent check).** Deleting 12 vertices from the Erdős–Rényi polarity
graph ER₇ (57 vertices, degrees 7 and 8) leaves a C₄-free graph on 45 vertices with
δ = 6 = 45 − 39, so f(39) ≥ 46. An explicit edge list is in `lower_bound_45.json`;
`verify.py` re-checks it.

So everything hinges on: **is there a C₄-free graph on 46 vertices with minimum
degree 7?**

## 2. Notation and the counting identity

Let G be C₄-free with δ(G) ≥ 7 on 46 vertices. For a vertex v put

* m_v = number of edges inside N(v) = number of triangles through v;
* f_v = number of vertices at distance ≥ 3 from v;
* S_u = N(u) ∖ N[v] for u ∈ N(v).

C₄-freeness gives two facts used everywhere:

* **(F1)** each u ∈ N(v) has at most one neighbour inside N(v) — otherwise two of them
  together with v close a C₄. So N(v) induces a matching, and m_v ≤ ⌊d(v)/2⌋.
* **(F2)** for u ≠ u′ in N(v) the sets S_u and S_{u′} are disjoint — a common vertex w
  gives the C₄ u w u′ v.

Counting V by distance from v (using (F1), (F2)):

  |V| = 1 + d(v) + Σ_{u∈N(v)} (d(u) − 1 − ε_u) + f_v,  ε_u = [u has a neighbour in N(v)],

and since Σ_u ε_u = 2m_v,

> **(★)  Σ_{u ∈ N(v)} d(u) = |V| − 1 + 2m_v − f_v.**

## 3. Lemma 1 — G is 7-regular

Let d = d(v). By (★), Σ_{u∈N(v)} d(u) = 45 + 2m_v − f_v ≤ 45 + d, using 2m_v ≤ d
and f_v ≥ 0. Every neighbour has degree ≥ 7, so 7d ≤ 45 + d, giving d ≤ 7.5, i.e.
d = 7. ∎

## 4. Lemma 2 — the local parameters

With every degree equal to 7, (★) becomes 49 = 45 + 2m_v − f_v, so

> **f_v = 2m_v − 4, and m_v ∈ {2, 3} for every vertex.**

Also each v sees exactly 7·6 = 42 distinct other vertices at distance ≤ 2, so the
"no common neighbour" graph D is **3-regular**, and with A the adjacency matrix

  **A² = 6I + J − D,  AD = DA.**

## 5. Lemma 3 — the key exclusion

> **Lemma.** Let k be odd and let G be k-regular and C₄-free. Then no vertex v can have
> f_v = 0 together with m_v ≥ 1.

*Proof.* Let u₁ ∈ N(v) be matched inside N(v), to u₂ say. Since f_v = 0, the sets
S_{u₁},…,S_{u_k} partition the set of vertices at distance 2 from v, and by (F1)
|S_{u_i}| = k − 2 if u_i is matched, k − 1 otherwise. Let x ∈ S_{u₁}. Then:

1. x has exactly one neighbour in N(v), namely u₁ — two neighbours in N(v) would give
   a C₄ through v — and x ≁ v. So x has exactly k − 1 neighbours at distance 2 from v.
2. x has at most one neighbour in any S_{u_j}: two of them, y and y′, would have the
   two common neighbours u_j and x.
3. x has **no** neighbour in S_{u₂}: y ∈ S_{u₂} with x ~ y gives the 4-cycle
   u₁ x y u₂ (all four vertices distinct, and u₂ ~ u₁).

By 1–3 the k − 1 neighbours of x at distance 2 are distributed one each among the
k − 1 blocks S_{u₁}, S_{u₃}, S_{u₄}, …, S_{u_k}. In particular **every** x ∈ S_{u₁} has
exactly one neighbour inside S_{u₁}, i.e. G[S_{u₁}] is a perfect matching on
|S_{u₁}| = k − 2 vertices. For k odd this is impossible. ∎

**Consequences for k = 7** (here f_v = 0 means |V| = 50 − 2m_v):

* |V| = 44 forces m_v = 3 for all v, so Lemma 3 applies: **there is no 7-regular
  C₄-free graph on 44 vertices.** (An elementary re-proof of f(37) = 44.)
* |V| = 46: **no vertex has m_v = 2.**
* |V| = 48: no vertex has m_v = 1.
* More generally, for odd k there is no k-regular C₄-free graph on k² − k + 2 vertices,
  which yields Parsons' bound f(q²+1) ≤ q² + q + 2 by an elementary argument.

## 6. Structure theorem for the surviving case

Lemmas 2 and 3 together: if a C₄-free graph on 46 vertices with δ ≥ 7 exists then

> **m_v = 3 and f_v = 2 for every vertex v.**

Hence:

* G has exactly 46 triangles; they are pairwise edge-disjoint and every vertex lies in
  exactly 3 of them;
* every vertex meets exactly 7 − 2·3 = 1 edge lying in no triangle, so the triangle-free
  edges form a **perfect matching M**, and **G = (46 edge-disjoint triangles) ⊔ M**;
* "distance exactly 3" is a **2-regular** relation; D = M ⊔ D_far is the cubic
  deficiency graph.

Fix a root v and relabel:

* N(v) = {u₁,…,u₇}, with u₁u₂, u₃u₄, u₅u₆ ∈ E and u₇ unmatched; M(v) = u₇;
* blocks S₁,…,S₆ (5 vertices, inducing a 2-matching with unmatched vertex z_i = M(u_i)),
  S₇ (6 vertices, inducing a perfect matching), and F = {p, q} at distance 3 from v;
* **no edges between partner blocks** S₁–S₂, S₃–S₄, S₅–S₆ (Lemma 3, step 3);
* every vertex has at most one neighbour in each block (Lemma 3, step 2);
* p and q have all their neighbours inside the blocks (plus possibly each other), at
  most one per block;
* **z_i is adjacent to p or q**: z_i has no neighbour in S_i nor in the partner block, so
  its 6 neighbours besides u_i must fit into only 5 blocks.

## 7. Canonical case split

Relabelling symmetry is used to pin down N(p):

* swapping p and q (legitimate because z₁ is adjacent to at least one of them) gives
  **WLOG p ~ z₁**, hence p's neighbour in S₁ is exactly z₁;
* the stabiliser of the fixed structure inside S_i (i = 2,…,6) has order 8 and is
  transitive on the four matched vertices while fixing z_i, so **WLOG p's neighbour in
  S_i, if any, is a fixed matched vertex or z_i** — three possibilities per block;
* the stabiliser inside S₇ has order 48 and is transitive, so **WLOG p's neighbour in
  S₇, if any, is one fixed vertex** — two possibilities.

These groups act on pairwise disjoint vertex sets and preserve everything already fixed,
so the reduction is sound. Together with the two possibilities p ~ q / p ≁ q (which fix
|N(p) ∩ blocks| = 6 or 7) this leaves exactly **144 canonical cases**.

## 7b. A spectral certificate that removes the search

On the T = 46 branch the fifth moment is *exactly* computable, and it closes the problem
without any exhaustive search.

With P = 6I − D (D the cubic deficiency graph, PJ = 3J):
A⁴ = P² + 52J, A⁵ = AP² + 364J, hence

  Σθ⁵ = tr(A⁵) − 7⁵ = −12·tr(AD) + tr(AD²) + 7·46·52 − 7⁵ = tr(AD²) − 615,

using tr(AD) = 2·#(triangle-free edges) = 2·23 = 46.

For an edge uv, N_D(u) = {M(u)} ∪ F_u where F_u are the two vertices at distance 3.
M(u) ≠ M(v) (M is a matching) and M(u) ∉ F_v (M(u) ~ u ~ v puts it within distance 2),
so **N_D(u) ∩ N_D(v) = F_u ∩ F_v**. Summing over edges and swapping the order,

  Σ_{uv∈E} |F_u ∩ F_v| = Σ_w #{edges inside {x : d(x,w) = 3}} = κ,

where κ := #{w : the two vertices at distance 3 from w are adjacent}, because the
distance-3 relation is 2-regular. Hence **Σθ⁵ = 2κ − 615 with 0 ≤ κ ≤ 46**, i.e.

  **Σθ⁵ ∈ [−615, −523].**

Feeding Σθ = −7, Σθ² = 273, Σθ³ = −67, Σθ⁴ = 1785, Σθ⁵ ∈ [−615, −523] and
|θ| ∈ [√3, 3] into the moment LP gives

  **N₊ ∈ [21.17558, 21.80700]**  (stable over grids of 4k–240k points),

and N₊ (a count of eigenvalues) is an integer. Contradiction. The 6th moment is not
needed.

*The grid LP is only a heuristic* — discretising the support **shrinks** the feasible set
and can report an artificially narrow window. The proof proper is the LP dual, two
explicit rational degree-5 polynomials:

  p(x) = 423x⁵/200000 + 19x⁴/15625 − 8719x³/200000 − 4087x²/250000
         + 390143x/1000000 + 277973/500000
  q(x) = 11x⁵/5000 − 203x⁴/250000 − 45053x³/1000000 + 5561x²/500000
         + 98979x/250000 + 114939/250000

with p ≥ 1 on [√3,3], p ≥ 0 on [−3,−√3] (giving N₊ ≤ 4361769/200000 = 21.808845), and
q ≤ 1 on [√3,3], q ≤ 0 on [−3,−√3] (giving N₊ ≥ 4234009/200000 = 21.170045). The x⁵
coefficient is positive in both, so M₅ is taken at −523 for the upper bound and at −615
for the lower. **No integer lies in [21.170045, 21.808845]**, and N₊ counts eigenvalues.

All four inequalities are verified exactly in `check_certificates.py` by real-root
isolation (`CRootOf`) plus endpoint evaluation in ℚ(√3). Each polynomial has exactly one
real root, and each root falls just outside its interval:

| condition | unique real root | interval | margin at the tight endpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| p − 1 ≥ 0 | 1.73145377 | [√3, 3], √3 = 1.73205081 | p(√3) − 1 = 278393√3/10⁶ − 241077/5·10⁵ ≈ 3.68e−5 |
| p ≥ 0 | −3.00002746 | [−3, −√3] | p(−3) = 1e−6 |
| 1 − q ≥ 0 | 3.00002019 | [√3, 3] | 1 − q(3) = 1e−6 |
| −q ≥ 0 | −1.73021295 | [−3, −√3] | −q(−√3) = 280557√3/10⁶ − 242907/5·10⁵ ≈ 1.25e−4 |

> **Trap for verifiers.** Do **not** check these on a rational super-interval such as
> [173/100, 3] ⊇ [√3, 3]. The certificate is tight at √3: p(1.73) − 1 = −9.0e−5 < 0 and
> −q(−1.73) = −1.45e−5 < 0, so the naive rational-endpoint check *fails* even though the
> certificate is valid. Verification must use the true algebraic endpoints ±√3.

The identity Σθ⁵ = −2(k−1)tr(AD) + tr(AD²) + kn[2(k−1−z)+n] − k⁵ is verified on
Petersen (10,3), Heawood (14,3), Hoffman–Singleton (50,7) and the found (15,4), (26,5),
(34,6) graphs in `verify_m5.py`.

## 8. The finite check (now an independent cross-check, not the proof)

Each case is encoded exactly in propositional logic (`caseB2.py`): edge variables,
degree-exactly-7 cardinality constraints, common-neighbour indicator variables with an
at-most-one constraint per vertex pair (C₄-freeness), and the constraint that every
vertex meets exactly one triangle-free edge. Each instance has ≈ 27 000 variables and
≈ 220 000 clauses. **All 144 instances are unsatisfiable** (CaDiCaL 1.9.5; per-instance
times 2 s – 424 s, about 3.5 CPU-hours in total). Full log: `results.jsonl`.

Additionally, the branch "z₁ adjacent to neither p nor q" — the fact underlying the
p ↔ q normalisation — is separately confirmed UNSAT by the solver in both p ~ q cases,
matching the hand proof in Section 6.

**Therefore no C₄-free graph on 46 vertices has minimum degree 7, and**

> ### R(C₄, K₁,₃₉) = 46, R(C₄, W₄₀) = 46.

## 9. Validation of the computation

1. **The encoding accepts genuine graphs at full scale.** The Hoffman–Singleton graph
   (7-regular, girth 5, 50 vertices) satisfies the CNF generated for (n,k,m) = (50,7,0)
   with zero conflicts against the hard-coded layout (`validate_encoding.py`).
2. **The Case-B-specific constraint is unit-tested** on graphs whose triangle-free-edge
   counts are computed directly — Hoffman–Singleton (all counts 7) and the 34-vertex
   6-regular C₄-free graph (counts 0 and 2). The encoder accepts the true counts and
   rejects perturbed ones (`validate_yconstraint.py`).
3. **The general encoding reproduces every neighbouring known value**: it finds
   k-regular C₄-free graphs on (10,3), (15,4), (26,5), (34,6) and reports non-existence
   for (14,4), matching f(10) = 14, f(11) = 16, f(21) = 27, f(28) = 35.
4. **Lemma 3 is confirmed independently by the solver**: the m = 2 branch at n = 46 and
   the m = 1 branch at n = 48 are both UNSAT within seconds, exactly as the lemma
   predicts.
5. **Cross-solver check**: a re-run of the 144 instances under Kissat was started
   but not completed (Kissat via these bindings proved far slower on these
   instances); no cross-solver results are claimed. The SAT computation is
   corroboration of the spectral proof, not a load-bearing component.

## 10. What is *not* claimed

* The final step is a machine check, not a human-surveyable proof. It is reproducible
  and every instance can be made to emit a DRAT certificate, but no such certificate has
  been checked here.
* Novelty is asserted only as "no prior resolution located": the value is listed as open
  in the June 2026 arXiv version of Boza's paper and in Radziszowski's dynamic survey,
  and searches found no later resolution — but that is not a guarantee.
* This settles one exact small Ramsey number. It does **not** bear on Erdős Problem #552
  (whether R(C₄,K₁,ₙ) ≤ n + √n − c infinitely often for every c), which is the
  genuinely large open question in this family.
